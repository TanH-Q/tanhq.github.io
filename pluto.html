What Is Pluto?

Pluto is a dwarf planet in the Kuiper Belt beyond Neptune. It is smaller than Earth's Moon and travels around the Sun on a distant, elliptical orbit.

Pluto Facts

  • Type: Dwarf planet
  • Location: Kuiper Belt
  • Discovery: 1930
  • Discoverer: Clyde Tombaugh
  • Average distance from the Sun: About 5.9 billion kilometers
  • Diameter: About 2,377 kilometers
  • Known moons: Five
  • Length of a Pluto day: About 6.4 Earth days
  • Length of a Pluto year: About 248 Earth years

Surface and Atmosphere

Pluto's surface contains frozen nitrogen, methane, and carbon monoxide. Its landscape includes icy plains, mountains, and craters. A thin atmosphere surrounds the planet, consisting mainly of nitrogen with smaller amounts of other gases.

Pluto is extremely cold because it is so far from the Sun. Its surface and atmosphere also change as Pluto moves along its long orbit.

Why Is Pluto a Dwarf Planet?

In 2006, the International Astronomical Union classified Pluto as a dwarf planet. Pluto orbits the Sun and is nearly round, but it has not cleared its orbital neighborhood of other objects.
