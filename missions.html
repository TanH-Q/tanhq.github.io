Exploring the Unknown

Pluto is extremely far from Earth, making close-up exploration challenging. For many years, scientists studied Pluto using telescopes. NASA's New Horizons spacecraft later provided the first close-up views of Pluto and its moons.

New Horizons

  • Launch date: January 19, 2006
  • Pluto flyby: July 14, 2015
  • Space agency: NASA
  • Main goal: Study Pluto, its moons, and the outer solar system

New Horizons traveled for more than nine years before passing Pluto. During the flyby, it collected images and scientific measurements that revealed mountains, icy plains, and details about Pluto's thin atmosphere.

What Did Scientists Discover?

Complex Landscapes

Images revealed mountains made largely of water ice and broad plains containing frozen nitrogen.

Active Surface Processes

Pluto's surface features suggest that its icy landscape has changed over time.

A Better View of Charon

The flyby also revealed striking surface features on Charon, Pluto's largest moon.
