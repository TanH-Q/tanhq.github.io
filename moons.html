A Family of Moons

Pluto has five known natural satellites: Charon, Styx, Nix, Kerberos, and Hydra. Charon is the largest and is so large compared with Pluto that the two bodies are sometimes described as a double dwarf-planet system.

Meet the Five Moons

Charon

Discovered in 1978, Charon is Pluto's largest moon. It is about half Pluto's diameter and has a large influence on the motion of the two bodies.

Nix

Discovered in 2005, Nix is a small, irregularly shaped moon. It was named after Nyx, the Greek goddess of the night.

Hydra

Also discovered in 2005, Hydra is an irregular moon named after the many-headed creature from Greek mythology.

Kerberos

Discovered in 2011, Kerberos is a small moon named after the mythical three-headed guard of the underworld.

Styx

Discovered in 2012, Styx is the smallest of Pluto's five known moons. Its name comes from a river associated with the Greek underworld.

How Were the Moons Studied?

NASA's New Horizons spacecraft photographed Pluto and several of its moons during its 2015 flyby. These observations helped scientists learn more about their shapes, surfaces, and motions.
